# DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria: Bipolar and Related Disorders

## 1. Bipolar I Disorder
- **ICD-10-CM Codes**: F31.11 - F31.9
- **DSM-5 Code Category**: Bipolar and Related Disorders

### Diagnostic Criteria
- **Core Requirement**: Necessary to have met criteria for at least **one manic episode**. Major depressive or hypomanic episodes are common but not strictly required for Bipolar I diagnosis (though almost all individuals experience them).

### Criteria for Manic Episode
- **Criterion A**: A distinct period of abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive, or irritable mood and abnormally and persistently increased goal-directed activity or energy, lasting **at least 1 week** and present most of the day, nearly every day (or any duration if hospitalization is necessary).
- **Criterion B**: During the period of mood disturbance and increased energy, **three (or more)** of the following symptoms (four if the mood is only irritable) are present to a significant degree:
  1. **Inflated self-esteem or grandiosity**.
  2. **Decreased need for sleep** (e.g., feels rested after only 3 hours of sleep).
  3. **More talkative than usual** or pressure to keep talking.
  4. **Flight of ideas** or subjective experience that thoughts are racing.
  5. **Distractibility** (i.e., attention too easily drawn to unimportant or irrelevant external stimuli).
  6. **Increase in goal-directed activity** (either socially, at work or school, or sexually) or **psychomotor agitation**.
  7. **Excessive involvement in activities that have a high potential for painful consequences** (e.g., engaging in unrestrained buying sprees, sexual indiscretions, or foolish business investments).
- **Criterion C**: The mood disturbance is sufficiently severe to cause **marked impairment in social or occupational functioning**, or to necessitate **hospitalization** to prevent harm to self or others, or there are **psychotic features**.

### Statistical & Epidemiological Metrics
- **12-Month Prevalence**: **0.6%** in U.S. adults.
- **Lifetime Prevalence**: **1.0%**.
- **Male-to-Female Ratio**: Approximately **1:1** (equal prevalence across sexes).
- **Mean Age of Onset**: **18 years** for Bipolar I.
- **Risk Factors**: Family history is one of the strongest risk factors (10-fold higher risk among first-degree relatives of individuals with Bipolar I or II).
- **Suicide Risk**: Lifetime risk of suicide is estimated to be at least 15 times higher than in the general population; accounts for ~1/4 of all completed suicides in some clinical samples.

## 2. Bipolar II Disorder
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F31.81
- **DSM-5 Code Category**: Bipolar and Related Disorders

### Diagnostic Criteria
- **Core Requirement**: Necessary to have met criteria for at least **one hypomanic episode** AND at least **one major depressive episode**.
- **Crucial Rule**: There has **never been a manic episode**.

### Criteria for Hypomanic Episode
- Distinct period of elevated, expansive, or irritable mood and increased activity/energy lasting **at least 4 consecutive days**.
- Includes 3 or more manic symptoms (Criterion B above).
- **Key Differentiation from Mania**: The episode is *not* severe enough to cause marked impairment in functioning or hospitalization, and there are *no psychotic features*.

### Statistical & Epidemiological Metrics
- **12-Month Prevalence**: **0.8%** in the U.S.
- **Lifetime Prevalence**: **1.1%**.
- **Sex Ratio**: Slightly higher in females, particularly for rapid cycling and comorbid eating disorders.
- **Average Age of Onset**: Mid-20s.

## 3. Cyclothymic Disorder
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F34.0
- **Core Criteria**: For at least **2 years** (1 year in children/adolescents), numerous periods with hypomanic symptoms that do not meet criteria for a hypomanic episode and numerous periods with depressive symptoms that do not meet criteria for a major depressive episode. Symptoms present for at least half the time and not absent for more than 2 months at a time.

## 4. Differential Diagnosis Guidelines for Bipolar Disorders
1. **Bipolar I vs. Bipolar II**: Bipolar I requires at least one full Manic episode (marked impairment, psychosis, or hospitalization). Bipolar II requires Hypomania (no marked impairment, no hospitalization, no psychosis) PLUS Major Depression.
2. **Bipolar Disorder vs. Major Depressive Disorder**: Inspect lifetime history for elevated mood/energy periods. Misdiagnosing Bipolar Depression as MDD can result in antidepressant-induced manic switching.
3. **Bipolar Disorder vs. ADHD**: ADHD features chronic, trait-like inattention/hyperactivity beginning in childhood; Bipolar mania features episodic spikes in energy, grandiosity, and reduced need for sleep.
4. **Bipolar Disorder vs. Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD)**: BPD mood shifts happen rapidly within hours in response to interpersonal stressors; Bipolar episodes last days, weeks, or months with distinct affective shifts.
