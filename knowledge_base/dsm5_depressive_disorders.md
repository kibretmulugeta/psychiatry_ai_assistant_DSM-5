# DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria: Depressive Disorders

## 1. Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)
- **ICD-10-CM Codes**: F32.0 - F32.9 (Single Episode), F33.0 - F33.9 (Recurrent)
- **DSM-5 Code Category**: Depressive Disorders

### Diagnostic Criteria A (Symptom Threshold)
Five (or more) of the following symptoms have been present during the same **2-week period** and represent a change from previous functioning; at least one of the symptoms is either **(1) depressed mood** or **(2) loss of interest or pleasure (anhedonia)**.

1. **Depressed mood** most of the day, nearly every day, as indicated by subjective report (e.g., feels sad, empty, hopeless) or observation by others. (In children/adolescents, can be irritable mood).
2. **Markedly diminished interest or pleasure** in all, or almost all, activities most of the day, nearly every day.
3. **Significant weight loss when not dieting or weight gain** (e.g., a change of more than 5% of body weight in a month), or decrease or increase in appetite nearly every day.
4. **Insomnia or hypersomnia** nearly every day.
5. **Psychomotor agitation or retardation** nearly every day (observable by others, not merely subjective feelings of restlessness or being slowed down).
6. **Fatigue or loss of energy** nearly every day.
7. **Feelings of worthlessness or excessive or inappropriate guilt** (which may be delusional) nearly every day (not merely self-reproach or guilt about being sick).
8. **Diminished ability to think or concentrate, or indecisiveness**, nearly every day.
9. **Recurrent thoughts of death** (not just fear of dying), recurrent suicidal ideation without a specific plan, or a suicide attempt or a specific plan for committing suicide.

### Diagnostic Criteria B, C, D, E
- **Criterion B**: Symptoms cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning.
- **Criterion C**: The episode is not attributable to the physiological effects of a substance (e.g., drug of abuse, medication) or another medical condition.
- **Criterion D**: The occurrence of the major depressive episode is not better explained by schizoaffective disorder, schizophrenia, schizophreniform disorder, delusional disorder, or other specified/unspecified schizophrenia spectrum disorders.
- **Criterion E**: There has never been a manic episode or a hypomanic episode (unless manic/hypomanic-like episodes are substance-induced or due to medical conditions).

### Diagnostic Specifiers
- With anxious distress
- With mixed features
- With melancholic features
- With atypical features
- With psychotic features (mood-congruent or mood-incongruent)
- With catatonia
- With peripartum onset
- With seasonal pattern (Seasonal Affective Disorder)

### Statistical & Epidemiological Metrics
- **12-Month Prevalence**: Approximately **7.0%** in the United States.
- **Age Differences**: Prevalence in individuals aged 18-29 is 3 times higher than in individuals aged 60 or older.
- **Sex Ratio**: Females experience 1.5 to 3-fold higher rates than males starting in early adolescence.
- **Peak Age of Onset**: Mid-20s, though onset can occur at any age.
- **Heritability**: Approximately 40% heritability (first-degree family members have 2-4x risk).
- **Comorbidity**: Frequently co-occurs with Anxiety Disorders (GAD, Panic), Substance Use Disorders, PTSD, and Borderline Personality Disorder.

## 2. Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia)
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F34.1
- **DSM-5 Code Category**: Depressive Disorders

### Diagnostic Criteria
- **Criterion A**: Depressed mood for most of the day, for more days than not, as indicated by subjective account or observation by others, for **at least 2 years** (at least 1 year in children/adolescents, where mood can be irritable).
- **Criterion B**: Presence, while depressed, of two (or more) of the following:
  1. Poor appetite or overeating.
  2. Insomnia or hypersomnia.
  3. Low energy or fatigue.
  4. Low self-esteem.
  5. Poor concentration or difficulty making decisions.
  6. Feelings of hopelessness.
- **Criterion C**: During the 2-year period (1 year for children/adolescents), the individual has never been without the symptoms in Criteria A and B for more than **2 months** at a time.
- **Criterion D**: Criteria for major depressive disorder may be continuously present for 2 years.
- **Criterion E**: No history of manic or hypomanic episodes, or cyclothymic disorder.

### Statistical & Epidemiological Metrics
- **12-Month Prevalence**: ~1.5% in the U.S.
- **Lifetime Prevalence**: ~0.5% for chronic PDD.
- **Course**: Insidious onset, chronic course. Early onset (before age 21) is associated with higher likelihood of comorbid personality disorders and substance use disorders.

## 3. Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD)
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F34.81
- **Age Range**: Diagnosed between ages 6 and 18; onset must be before age 10.
- **Core Feature**: Severe recurrent temper outbursts manifested verbally and/or behaviorally out of proportion in intensity or duration to the situation or provocation, occurring on average 3 or more times per week in at least 2 settings. Mood between outbursts is persistently irritable/angry most of the day.

## 4. Differential Diagnosis Guidelines for Depressive Disorders
1. **Depressive Disorder vs. Manic/Bipolar Episodes**: If history includes at least one lifetime manic or hypomanic episode, diagnosis shifts to Bipolar I or Bipolar II Disorder.
2. **Depressive Disorder vs. Normal Grief/Bereavement**: Grief comes in waves with preserved self-esteem; MDD presents persistent non-remitting depressed mood with worthlessness and self-loathing.
3. **Depressive Disorder vs. Substance/Medication-Induced Mood Disorder**: Depression symptoms linked strictly to intoxication or withdrawal (e.g., alcohol, opioids, stimulants, corticosteroids).
4. **Depressive Disorder vs. Hypothyroidism / Neurological Conditions**: Rule out medical causes (e.g., hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency, stroke) via clinical workup.
