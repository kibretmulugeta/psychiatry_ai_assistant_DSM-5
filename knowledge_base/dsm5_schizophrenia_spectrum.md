# DSM-5 Diagnostic Criteria: Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders

## 1. Schizophrenia
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F20.9
- **DSM-5 Code Category**: Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders

### Diagnostic Criteria
- **Criterion A (Symptom Criteria)**: **Two (or more)** of the following, each present for a significant portion of time during a **1-month period** (or less if successfully treated). At least one of these must be **(1), (2), or (3)**:
  1. **Delusions** (fixed false beliefs not amenable to change in light of conflicting evidence).
  2. **Hallucinations** (perception-like experiences that occur without an external stimulus, most commonly auditory).
  3. **Disorganized speech** (e.g., frequent derailment or incoherence, word salad).
  4. **Grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior**.
  5. **Negative symptoms** (i.e., diminished emotional expression or avolition/anhedonia/alogia).
- **Criterion B (Functional Impairment)**: For a significant portion of the time since the onset of the disturbance, level of functioning in one or more major areas, such as work, interpersonal relations, or self-care, is markedly below the level achieved prior to the onset.
- **Criterion C (Duration)**: Continuous signs of the disturbance persist for **at least 6 months**. This 6-month period must include at least 1 month of symptoms (or less if treated) that meet Criterion A (active-phase symptoms) and may include periods of prodromal or residual symptoms.
- **Criterion D (Schizoaffective & Mood Exclusion)**: Schizoaffective disorder and depressive or bipolar disorder with psychotic features have been ruled out.
- **Criterion E (Substance/Medical Exclusion)**: The disturbance is not attributable to physiological effects of a substance or another medical condition.

### Statistical & Epidemiological Metrics
- **Lifetime Prevalence**: **0.3% - 0.7%** worldwide.
- **Sex Ratio**: Slightly higher male-to-female ratio (1.4:1). Males typically present with earlier onset and more severe negative symptoms.
- **Age of Onset**: Typically **late adolescence to mid-30s**. Peak onset for males is early-to-mid 20s; for females, late 20s.
- **Comorbidity**: Substance Use Disorders (over 50% smoke cigarettes heavily; alcohol and cannabis use disorders are frequent), Anxiety Disorders, OCD.
- **Suicide Risk**: Approximately **5% - 6%** of individuals with schizophrenia die by suicide, and 20% attempt suicide.

## 2. Schizoaffective Disorder
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F25.0 (Bipolar Type) / F25.1 (Depressive Type)
- **DSM-5 Code Category**: Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders

### Core Criteria
- An uninterrupted period of illness during which there is a major mood episode (Major Depressive or Manic) concurrent with Criterion A of Schizophrenia.
- **Crucial Diagnostic Requirement**: Delusions or hallucinations for **at least 2 weeks** in the absence of a major mood episode during the lifetime duration of the illness.
- Symptoms that meet criteria for a major mood episode are present for the majority of the total duration of the active and residual portions of the illness.

---

## 3. Delusional Disorder
- **ICD-10-CM Code**: F22
- **Core Criteria**: Presence of **one (or more) delusions** with a duration of **1 month or longer**. Criterion A for Schizophrenia has *never* been met (hallucinations, if present, are not prominent and are related to the delusional theme). Functioning is not markedly impaired, and behavior is not obviously bizarre or odd outside the delusion.
- **Subtypes**: Erotomanic, Grandiose, Jealous, Persecutory, Somatic, Mixed, Unspecified.

---

## 4. Differential Diagnosis Guidelines for Psychotic Disorders
1. **Schizophrenia vs. Schizoaffective Disorder**: In Schizophrenia, mood symptoms are brief relative to psychotic duration. In Schizoaffective, psychotic symptoms persist for ≥2 weeks *without* mood symptoms, but mood symptoms cover >50% of the total illness duration.
2. **Schizophrenia vs. Major Depression with Psychotic Features**: In Major Depression with Psychotic Features, psychosis occurs *only* during the active major depressive episode.
3. **Schizophrenia vs. Brief Psychotic Disorder**: Brief Psychotic Disorder lasts at least 1 day but *less than 1 month* with full return to premorbid functioning.
4. **Schizophrenia vs. Substance-Induced Psychosis**: Rule out amphetamines, cocaine, phencyclidine (PCP), cannabis, or withdrawal from alcohol/sedatives.
